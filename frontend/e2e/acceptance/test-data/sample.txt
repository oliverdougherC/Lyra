Introduction to Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics is the branch of physics that studies heat, work, temperature,
and their relation to energy, entropy, and the physical properties of matter.

First Law of Thermodynamics
The first law states that energy cannot be created or destroyed in an isolated
system.  The change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done:
delta-U equals Q minus W.

Second Law of Thermodynamics
The second law states that the total entropy of an isolated system can only
increase over time.  Heat naturally flows from a hotter body to a cooler one.

Third Law of Thermodynamics
The third law states that as a system approaches absolute zero, its entropy
approaches a minimum value.

Key Definitions
Internal energy is the total kinetic and potential energy of the particles in
a system.  Entropy is a measure of the number of accessible microstates.
Enthalpy equals internal energy plus pressure times volume (H = U + PV).
Free energy determines whether a process is spontaneous at constant
temperature and pressure.
